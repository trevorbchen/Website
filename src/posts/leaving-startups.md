I started Ascade in June 2025 with a close friend, Pius, and my time there ended in May 2026. We were accepted into Y Combinator's F25 batch and I chose not to go, because I wanted to do research. Nothing went badly. I like startups and expect to join one again.

## What we built

The earliest prototypes in my files are from September and October 2025. The first was a pipeline that turned plain-English specifications into formal postconditions: you write "sort a list of integers" and it produces the mathematical statement a correct sort must satisfy, using a language model and an SMT solver (Z3) to check it. The second was a desktop app for designing a program's behavior as a graph over its abstract syntax tree and exporting contracts from it.

Those grew into formally verified software. We shipped a verified payment processor that reached $25K in monthly revenue, won a DARPA ERIS contract and other federal work on verified access control for AI agents, and trained a small 8B model that set the state of the art on formal-verification benchmarks, ahead of much larger frontier models. I wrote about the idea behind it [here](verification-loop.html).

## What I liked

You move fast, make decisions with half the information, ship, and learn within a week whether you were right. The loop between an idea and a customer's reaction is short, and I learned a lot from it.

## Why I left

I wanted to see how far I could push my own thinking. At the startup the problems were clear and so were the ways to make money, and the work was exploring branches that were already visible and finding the one that paid best. In research I get to ask open-ended questions and follow them, and sometimes to grow a branch nobody has tried. The areas I work on, diffusion models, AI for science and increasingly the theory under them, are ones where finding the right question is most of the work. A result there also either holds up or it doesn't, which I like.

## Next

I expect to come back. I see this as the long way around: push on research until I find my limits, then bring what I've learned to a team that needs it. Pius is still one of my closest friends, which matters more to me than the plan did.
