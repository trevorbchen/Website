I broke an AI-image detector (both ways)

2026-08-03 · AI safetyred-teaming

Pangram is one of the most popular AI-content detectors, and a lot of people online treat its verdict as final. I wanted to know how much weight that verdict can actually carry, so I spent some time poking at the image detector.

1. Ordinary upscaling looks "AI"

Upsampling a real image from 128×128 to 512×512 consistently got it flagged as AI-generated. That isn't an extreme transformation; plenty of everyday image pipelines upscale without the user ever knowing. My worry is less about this one setting and more about honest people getting flagged for using normal tools.

2. It can be pushed in both directions

Using out-of-distribution loss functions, I trained a VAE that makes real photos get flagged as AI, and a GAN-VAE that makes AI-generated images pass as human. Same family of models, opposite failure modes — which means a verdict alone can't be treated as proof either way.

I'm not releasing the code or the training details. A turnkey "make AI images undetectable" tool is a misuse risk, and the point was to show the gap, not to widen it. I shared details with Pangram directly.

3. Where the line should be

Pangram's co-founder replied to my thread with a fair question: if heavy upscaling hallucinates new detail, should that count as AI? I think there's a real taxonomy problem here — "AI-generated", "AI-edited", and "touched by a standard upscaler" probably shouldn't share one label.

Why I'm still optimistic about detection

Detecting AI has an infinite stream of data to learn from, while bypassing detectors is a very low-data, sparse-reward task.

Long term, I think detectors should keep the advantage (short of truly inhuman mimicry). AI detection is important work. For now, though, a detector's output should be one piece of evidence, not the final word.
