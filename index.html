hi

hi, i'm trevor.

I'm an undergrad at Caltech doing research on how generative models learn, what they represent inside, and how to use them to discover things.

Most of my work is on diffusion models and AI for science, especially biology: steering generative models toward molecules and proteins with very few expensive experiments, using reinforcement learning, active learning, and increasingly a closed loop with a real wet lab. I'm also interested in why diffusion models generalize instead of memorizing, in what's inside them (sparse autoencoders and interpretability), and in the theory underneath all of it. I started in physics and biology, so I like problems where the oracle is the real world.

I work with Yisong Yue and Pietro Perona at Caltech, Andreas Krause's group at ETH Zurich, and Frances Arnold's lab. Before that, I co-founded Ascade.

currently
  • Working on closed-loop discovery with Andreas Krause's group (ETH Zurich) and Frances Arnold's lab: language models propose hypotheses, generative models turn them into designs, and the wet lab says yes or no.
  • Writing two submissions: sparse-autoencoder dictionaries (CVPR) and why diffusion models memorize late (ICLR).
  • Learning reinforcement learning, evals, operating systems and distributed computing (my reading list).
